The company

Built on Grove Farm land in 2012

Koloa Zipline opened in 2012 on Grove Farm land above Koloa, the same ground that held Hawaii's first commercial sugar plantation. It is owned by Derek Green. The company has said the permits were the hardest part of building the course. Between the platforms the company has been replanting native trees, with the stated aim of a small botanical garden at every tower.

The lines run in a chain from the short Melaleuca and Uluhe runs up to Koa at 1,700 feet and Waita at 2,600, which the company calls the longest zipline on Kauai. Riders go seated with handlebars, hands-free, backward, or tandem with a child. On September 8, 2026 Hurricane Lowell tore through the south shore; the company has been running a four-line tour on the intact half and expects the full course back in November.

The one to book

The 4-line recovery tour, until the 8 lines return

Two and a quarter hours on the four lines the storm left intact, which the company says includes its best rides. Ground school first, then handlebar pulleys for the sightseeing lines, tandem rigs for kids from 7, and drinks at the end. Groups are capped at twelve.

The full eight-line tour is listed again from mid-November at $162, three and a half hours with snacks. Maximum weight is 280 pounds; wear closed-toe shoes and long shorts or pants.

Before you go

Know before you go

Where to check in

1 Mahaulepu Road, Koloa, 15 minutes before your time. Tours leave on schedule and do not wait for late guests; the cardholder shows the card at check-in.

Wear

Closed-toe shoes that can get wet, and pants or longer shorts. No flip-flops or open toes. Tank tops and short shorts are discouraged.

Bring

Bug spray, sunscreen, water and a camera you can strap on.

Limits

Ages 7 and up, 280 pounds maximum. Riders under 18 need an adult on the tour.

Weather

Rain does not cancel. Hazardous wind does; the company reschedules.

Questions

Good to know

How long are the lines?

Eight lines from 250 feet to 2,600 feet; the Waita line is the half-mile finale.

Is the full course open?

Not yet. Hurricane Lowell damaged part of the course in September 2026. A four-line tour runs now and the eight-line tour is listed from November 16.

Can kids ride?

Yes, from age 7, tandem with a guide or adult on the longer lines.

What does it cost?

The four-line tour is $139 and the eight-line tour $162 per person. Checked September 30, 2026.

What if it rains?

You still zipline. Only dangerous conditions cancel a tour.
