Swimming Holes in Hawaii: The Complete Safety Guide

By Jay Haven · Updated October 2026 · 13 min read Tours you can book here →

Hawaii's freshwater swimming holes — tiered waterfall pools, jungle-lined stream basins, and lava tidal pockets — are among the most beautiful in the world. They are also among the most dangerous. Kipu Falls is permanently closed after multiple drowning deaths. Oheo Gulch (the "Seven Sacred Pools") banned swimming in March 2025. Queen's Bath on Kauai's north shore has claimed more than ten lives since 2000. This guide covers which sites are worth visiting, which are off-limits, and exactly what precautions make the difference between a memorable day and a tragedy — before you ever leave your hotel.

Read before you swim: All freshwater in Hawaii carries a risk of leptospirosis, a potentially fatal bacterial infection. Never swim in freshwater within 48–72 hours of heavy rain. Do not enter with open wounds. Wear water shoes. Submerging your head increases exposure risk — avoid it.

Understanding the Risks

Leptospirosis

Leptospirosis is the most significant health risk at any freshwater site in Hawaii. The bacteria live in soil and water contaminated by the urine of infected animals — rats, mongooses, feral pigs, and deer. After heavy rain, contaminated runoff floods streams and pools, dramatically increasing bacteria concentrations. Symptoms typically appear 2–30 days after exposure and include high fever, severe headache, muscle pain, vomiting, and jaundice. Untreated cases can lead to kidney failure, meningitis, and death. The best protection: avoid freshwater contact for at least 48–72 hours after significant rainfall. Cover open cuts with waterproof bandaging before any water contact, or avoid swimming entirely.

Flash Floods

In Hawaii's mountains, heavy rain can begin miles upstream and descend with no visible warning. A sunny day at a waterfall pool does not mean the watershed upstream is dry. Stream levels can rise several feet in minutes. If you hear a roaring sound or see the water change color or volume suddenly, move immediately to high ground — do not wait to gather belongings. This is the primary cause of death at Oheo Gulch, which is why swimming is now prohibited there regardless of current sky conditions.

Ocean-Connected Pools

Some sites marketed as "swimming holes" — including Queen's Bath and portions of Waianapanapa — are coastal tidal features connected to the open ocean. Wave energy can enter these pools with no warning, sweeping swimmers onto lava rock. The swell height at these sites bears no relationship to the calm appearance of the pool itself. NOAA buoy readings and the Surfline swell forecast for north shore Kauai or Maui are the baseline checks before approaching any ocean-adjacent site.

Oahu Swimming Holes

Waimea Valley Waterfall Pool

The safest supervised freshwater swimming experience in Hawaii, Waimea Valley is a private botanical garden on Oahu's North Shore where a 3/4-mile paved path leads through manicured native plantings to a 45-foot waterfall and its broad pool. Life jackets are required and provided at no extra charge. Staff monitor the water level and close the pool after heavy rain; always call ahead: (808) 638-7766. Admission is $26 per adult, $13 for children 4–12. The garden is open daily 9 a.m.–5 p.m. This is the one site in Hawaii where a family with young children can swim at a waterfall without navigating an unmarked trail or flash flood risk.

Maunawili Falls

The most popular backcountry swimming hole on Oahu, Maunawili Falls sits in a Ko'olau valley above Kailua. The trailhead is at the end of Maunawili Road, and the route covers approximately 3.6 miles round-trip through a muddy, rooted jungle trail that can be genuinely slippery after rain. The falls cascade in two main tiers into a pool large enough to swim in. The site is on public land and free to access. Visit on a dry day only: the trail becomes treacherous and stream levels rise quickly after rain. The same general region hosts the broader best hikes on Oahu — pair Maunawili with the Kailua beach area for a full day.

Waimano Pool

A less-visited alternative to Maunawili, Waimano Pool is reached via the Waimano Trail — a roughly 7-mile round-trip route above Pearl City through an aqueduct system into a native forest valley. The pool is deep, shaded, and usually uncrowded. The distance and modest elevation gain make this a half-day commitment. Trailhead parking is at the end of Waimano Home Road.

Kauai Swimming Holes

Ho'opi'i Falls (Hoopii Falls)

On Kauai's east side above Kapaa, Ho'opi'i Falls is a two-tiered waterfall on Kapaa Stream accessible via a 2.2-mile round-trip trail through guava forest. The lower falls have a wide pool good for swimming; the upper falls are higher but the pool is shallower. The site is free to access and popular with local families. It is one of the more accessible freshwater swims on Kauai without requiring a long drive to the north shore. Avoid after rain — the trail parallels the stream and can flood at crossing points.

Queen's Bath

Queen's Bath is a naturally formed lava tidal pool on Kauai's north shore, near Princeville. In summer, when north-shore swells are at their lowest, the pool can be filled with clear water and tropical fish, and gentle conditions allow swimming. It is, in those moments, genuinely spectacular. But the site's reputation as a safe swimming hole is dangerous fiction: over 10 people have drowned at Queen's Bath since 2000, many of them in conditions that appeared calm. The access trail (0.7 miles one-way) descends through slippery roots; Kauai County has installed a gate that has been closed seasonally. The pool sits at the base of a lava bench; waves can arrive suddenly and without sound. Visitors have been swept in and unable to get out — the pool's smooth lava walls offer no handholds.

Queen's Bath protocol: Check the Surfline north shore swell forecast before leaving the house. Do not visit when surf exceeds 2 feet at the nearest buoy. Face the ocean at all times while on the lava bench. Never stand with your back to the water. Even with these precautions, this site carries real risk. Many experienced Hawaii hikers choose to skip it entirely.

The gate has been closed through winter months in recent seasons — check Hawaii DLNR for current access status before you go.

Kipu Falls — CLOSED

Kipu Falls was once one of Kauai's most well-known swimming holes. It is permanently closed. Following multiple drowning deaths, the landowner — Grove Farm — erected a physical barrier across the trail access point. Attempting to visit is criminal trespassing and the barrier is actively enforced. Any online content, social media post, or guidebook suggesting Kipu Falls is accessible is out of date. Do not go.

Maui Swimming Holes

Twin Falls

The first major waterfall stop on the Road to Hana, Twin Falls sits just past mile marker 2 in Huelo. The site is privately owned and operated; there is a small stand at the trailhead that may charge a nominal fee or accept donations. A 0.5-mile walk leads to the first set of pools; continuing further reaches additional cascades. The pools are good for wading and photography, and modest swimming is possible in the larger basins. The site is accessible and manageable for most fitness levels, which makes it consistently crowded. Arrive before 9 a.m. to beat tour buses. Flash flood risk applies — check the sky upstream.

Oheo Gulch (Seven Sacred Pools) — No Swimming

Oheo Gulch sits in the Kipahulu District of Haleakala National Park, about 10 miles past Hana on the Road to Hana loop. Seven tiered pools cascade down basalt ledges to the sea — it is one of the most photogenic freshwater features in Hawaii. Swimming, however, is prohibited as of March 2025. The National Park Service closed the pools due to the documented risks of flash flooding (the pools drain directly to the ocean and can rise catastrophically without warning) and leptospirosis. Violators may be cited.

The park entrance still provides access to the spectacular Pipiwai Trail (4 miles round-trip), which climbs through a bamboo forest to 400-foot Waimoku Falls — the best reason to visit the Kipahulu District. The pools remain worth viewing even without entering the water.

Still worth the drive: Even without swimming, the Oheo Gulch overlook and Pipiwai Trail are among the most memorable hikes in Hawaii. Budget 3–4 hours for the full trail to Waimoku Falls.

Waianapanapa State Park Coastal Pools

Waianapanapa's sea caves and lava pools are visually stunning and worth the reservation required to visit. But these are coastal features, not protected freshwater pools — they are directly influenced by open-ocean swell and surge. The Hawaii DLNR explicitly does not recommend swimming at Waianapanapa's shoreline. Visit for the black-sand beach, sea caves, King's Highway trail walk, and photography. The Road to Hana naturally incorporates Waianapanapa as a full stop.

Safety Checklist Before You Swim

  • Check the weather upstream, not just overhead. Clear skies at the pool mean nothing if it is raining in the mountains behind you.
  • Wait 48–72 hours after rain before entering any freshwater to let leptospirosis bacteria levels and flash flood risk subside.
  • Cover cuts and open wounds with waterproof bandaging, or do not enter at all.
  • Wear water shoes. Lava rock is sharp and slippery; bare feet invite lacerations that increase infection risk.
  • Never jump or dive into unknown pools — submerged rocks are invisible until it is too late.
  • Tell someone where you are going and when you expect to return. Cell service is absent at most backcountry sites.
  • At ocean-adjacent pools, face the water at all times. Never turn your back to waves.
  • If you hear a rushing sound or see water rise suddenly, move immediately to high ground. Do not try to retrieve gear.

What to Bring

  • 2 liters of water per person minimum — freshwater sites do not have drinking water
  • Insect repellent (mosquitoes are dense in valley trail corridors)
  • Water shoes or closed-toe shoes with grip soles
  • Quick-dry clothing and a dry change for the drive back
  • Waterproof bag or dry sack for phone and keys
  • First-aid kit including waterproof bandaging
  • Trekking poles for muddy trails (Maunawili, Waimano)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to swim in Hawaii's freshwater pools and streams?

It depends on the site and conditions. All freshwater in Hawaii carries some leptospirosis risk. Additional risks include flash floods, submerged rocks, and currents. Always check current conditions, avoid swimming after heavy rain, and do not enter with open cuts or wounds.

Is Oheo Gulch (Seven Sacred Pools) open for swimming?

No. As of March 2025, swimming at Oheo Gulch is prohibited by the National Park Service due to recurring flash flood and leptospirosis risks. The pools and Pipiwai Trail remain open for viewing and hiking, but entering the water is not allowed. Check the NPS website for current status.

Is Queen's Bath on Kauai safe to visit?

Queen's Bath is one of Hawaii's most dangerous coastal attractions — more than 10 drownings since 2000. Wave wash-overs occur with no warning in winter. Kauai County has periodically closed the access gate. Even in calm summer conditions, the site requires extreme caution. Never turn your back to the ocean there.

Is Kipu Falls open?

No. Kipu Falls on Kauai is permanently closed to the public. The landowner (Grove Farm) erected a physical barrier after multiple drowning deaths. Trespassing is a criminal offense. Do not attempt to visit.

What is leptospirosis and how can I avoid it in Hawaii?

Leptospirosis is a bacterial infection spread through water contaminated by animal urine. Symptoms include fever, headache, and muscle aches, appearing 2–30 days after exposure. Avoid all freshwater after heavy rain, cover open wounds, wear water shoes, and avoid submerging your head in streams or pools.

What is the best swimming hole on Oahu?

Maunawili Falls is the most popular backcountry swimming hole on Oahu — a 3.6-mile round-trip jungle trail to a tiered waterfall pool. Waimea Valley's supervised waterfall pool is the safest option for families. Waimano Pool is the best choice for solitude.

Can I swim at Waimea Valley waterfall on Oahu?

Yes, with conditions. Waimea Valley charges $26 per adult for admission to its botanical garden and waterfall pool. Life jackets are required. The pool closes after heavy rain — call (808) 638-7766 to confirm before visiting.

When is the best time to visit freshwater swimming holes in Hawaii?

The best time is during a dry stretch in summer (June–September). Avoid visiting within 48–72 hours of significant rainfall. Mornings are best before afternoon clouds and when trail footing is firmest.
