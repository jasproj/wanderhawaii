15 Best Things to Do in Waikiki in 2026

Updated August 2026 • 12 min read

Waikiki is a 2-mile strip of beach backed by high-rise hotels on Oahu's south shore. It's where most first-time Hawaii visitors stay, and it gets a bad rap from repeat visitors who treat it like a transit zone. That's a mistake. Waikiki has the most consistent surf break for beginners in Hawaii, some of the best food on the island, a free hula show three nights a week, and it puts you 30 minutes from Pearl Harbor and 45 minutes from the North Shore.

The key is knowing what's worth your time and what's overpriced filler. This guide covers both.

On the Water

1 Surf Lesson at Canoes Break

Waikiki is where Duke Kahanamoku popularized surfing for visitors over a century ago, and the break at Canoes is still one of the best places in the world to learn. The waves are long, slow, and forgiving. Multiple surf schools operate directly off the beach—look for ones with certified instructors and small group sizes (4:1 or better). A 2-hour group lesson runs $80–$120. Most beginners stand up on day one.

2 Turtle Canyon Snorkel Trip

Turtle Canyon is a shallow offshore reef just south of Waikiki where Hawaiian green sea turtles gather reliably. Catamaran tours depart directly from the beach—no driving, no harbor. A 2-hour trip includes gear and usually a guaranteed sighting policy. Better than Hanauma Bay if you want to skip the reservation system and crowds.

3 Sunset Catamaran Sail

Waikiki faces west—purpose-built for sunsets. Catamarans launch from the beach every evening, and watching the sun drop into the Pacific with Diamond Head behind you and a drink in hand is one of those things that's exactly as good as it sounds. The 2-hour sails run $55–$130 depending on whether drinks and food are included.

4 Parasailing

Parasailing puts you 600–1,200 feet above Waikiki with Diamond Head and the Honolulu skyline below. The flight is 8–10 minutes; the full boat experience runs about an hour. Multiple operators work from the Waikiki area. $80–$120 depending on altitude.

From $80

Soar above Waikiki with Diamond Head views. Solo and tandem options at multiple altitude levels. One of the more affordable parasailing operations on Oahu.

Duration: ~1 hour total • Departs: Waikiki

5 Stand-Up Paddleboarding

Waikiki's calm inside waters are ideal for SUP. Rent a board from beach concessions ($30–$40/hour) and paddle along the coast with Diamond Head ahead and the skyline behind. Early morning is best—flat water, fewer surfers. No experience needed; the inside lagoon area near the Hilton is particularly calm for beginners.

Land & Culture

6 Diamond Head Summit Hike

The most iconic hike in Waikiki is technically just outside it—Diamond Head crater is a 1.6-mile round trip with 560 feet of elevation gain. The reward is a 360-degree panorama of Waikiki, Honolulu, and the ocean. Takes about 1.5 hours total. Reservation required through the state parks system ($5 per person). Go early—heat builds fast and the parking lot fills by 9am.

Diamond Head tip: The hike includes steep stairs and a short tunnel. Bring water, a flashlight (for the tunnel), and sun protection. The crater floor parking lot is the trailhead. Uber or walk from Waikiki—it's only 2 miles from the heart of Waikiki.

7 Kuhio Beach Hula Show (Free)

Every Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday evening at 6:30pm, the City of Honolulu puts on a free hula performance at the Kuhio Beach Hula Mound. Torch-lighting ceremony, live Hawaiian music, and traditional and modern hula. It runs about an hour, starts at sunset, and draws locals and visitors alike. Bring a towel to sit on. One of the best free things in Waikiki—and one of the few cultural experiences that doesn't cost $100+.

8 Duke Kahanamoku Statue & Beach Walk

The bronze statue of Duke Kahanamoku—Olympic swimmer, surfing pioneer, and Hawaii's most famous waterman—stands with open arms at Kuhio Beach. It's the most photographed spot in Waikiki. Walk the beachfront promenade from the statue toward the Hilton Hawaiian Village for the full Waikiki beach experience. The walk is flat, about 1.5 miles one way, and passes every major stretch of sand.

9 Waikiki Aquarium

One of the oldest aquariums in the US (1904), small but well-curated with a focus on Hawaiian and Pacific marine life. The monk seal exhibit and living reef tank are highlights. $12 adults. Located at the east end of Waikiki near Kapiolani Park. Good for families—takes about an hour.

Budget tip: The Waikiki Aquarium, Honolulu Zoo, and Kapiolani Park are all clustered at the east end of Waikiki within walking distance of each other. Do all three in a morning for under $30 total.

10 Honolulu Zoo

A 42-acre zoo in Kapiolani Park with a good collection of tropical animals, an African savanna section, and a Komodo dragon exhibit. $19 adults, $11 kids. Not world-class, but solid for families with young kids who need a break from the beach. The Twilight Tours (select evenings) are a unique experience if timing works.

11 Kapiolani Park & Queen's Surf Beach

Kapiolani Park is 300 acres of green space at the foot of Diamond Head—jogging paths, banyan trees, picnic areas, and regular weekend events. Queen's Surf Beach on the park's ocean side is quieter than main Waikiki and has a good vibe—locals barbecuing, people watching surfers, families spread out on the grass. Free outdoor movie screenings happen on select Saturday evenings in summer.

12 International Market Place

Rebuilt in 2016 as an open-air shopping center with a massive banyan tree preserved in the courtyard. Higher-end than it used to be—think Tommy Bahama and Saks rather than the old-school stalls. The food hall on the third floor (The Street) has a good selection. The architecture around the banyan tree is genuinely impressive. Worth walking through even if you don't shop.

Beyond Waikiki (Easy Day Trips)

13 Pearl Harbor (30 min)

The USS Arizona Memorial and Battleship Missouri are 30 minutes from Waikiki by car or organized tour. This is the most significant historical site on Oahu and worth a full morning. Free timed-entry passes for the Arizona Memorial sell out—reserve at recreation.gov weeks in advance, or book a guided tour that includes transportation from Waikiki.

14 Kailua & Lanikai Beach (35 min)

Kailua is a windward-side beach town with dramatically different energy from Waikiki—quieter, more residential, with a gorgeous stretch of turquoise water. Lanikai Beach (a short walk from Kailua) consistently ranks among the best beaches in the US. The Lanikai Pillbox hike (see our Oahu hiking guide) is right here too. Rent a kayak and paddle to the Mokulua Islands.

15 North Shore (45 min)

The North Shore is Waikiki's opposite in every way—rural, laid-back, and centered on surf culture. In winter (November–February), Pipeline and Sunset Beach produce some of the biggest waves on Earth and the world's best surfers show up to ride them. In summer, the same beaches are calm enough for swimming. Haleiwa town has good food trucks, shave ice, and surf shops. A full day trip from Waikiki—see our North Shore guide.

Practical Tips

Skip the rental car for Waikiki days. Parking costs $35–$50/night at hotels. Everything in Waikiki is walkable. Rent a car only for the days you leave (Pearl Harbor, North Shore, Kailua, hikes).

Eat off the main strip. Kuhio Avenue (one block inland from the beach) has better food at lower prices than Kalakaua Avenue. Marukame Udon ($5 fresh noodles) regularly has a line out the door for good reason.

Best beaches by purpose: Main Waikiki Beach for convenience and people-watching. Fort DeRussy Beach (west end, near Hilton) for more space and calmer water. Queen's Surf Beach (east end, near the aquarium) for a local vibe.

When to go: Waikiki is good year-round. Winter (December–March) brings slightly larger surf and whale watching season. Summer is calmer water and longer days. September–November has the best combination of weather, prices, and fewer crowds.
