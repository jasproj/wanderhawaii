15 Best Things to Do on Oahu's North Shore in 2026

Updated August 2026 • 13 min read

The North Shore of Oahu is 7 miles of coastline that transforms completely between seasons. From November through February, Pipeline, Sunset Beach, and Waimea Bay produce waves that reach 40–60 feet on the face, and the world's best surfers come to ride them. The rest of the year, those same beaches flatten out into calm turquoise water you can float in with small children.

Beyond the surf, the North Shore is agricultural land, food trucks, a small surf town called Haleiwa, and a pace that makes Waikiki feel like Manhattan. It's a 45-minute drive from Honolulu and worth at least half a day. Here's what to do.

Surf & Beach

1 Watch Big Wave Surfing (Winter)

If you're on Oahu between November and February, check the surf forecast. When waves hit 15+ feet at Pipeline or Sunset Beach, pull over and watch—it's free and it's one of the most impressive athletic displays you'll ever see in person. The Banzai Pipeline breaks about 75 yards from shore, close enough to feel the energy. The Triple Crown of Surfing (November–December) brings the pros. Ehukai Beach Park has the best Pipeline viewing.

Wave watching tip: Check surfline.com or magicseaweed.com for the North Shore forecast. When the buoys read 15+ feet, it's worth the drive. Arrive early on big days—parking fills fast. Bring binoculars.

2 Waimea Bay

Waimea Bay is the North Shore's most famous beach and the original big-wave spot—Eddie Aikau made it legendary. In winter, waves can top 30 feet and The Eddie competition (invitation-only, held when waves reach 40+ feet) is one of surfing's most prestigious events. In summer, the bay is a calm swimming hole with a rock-jumping spot on the left side that locals use as a 25-foot cliff jump. The shore break is always powerful—respect it.

3 Sunset Beach

A long, wide stretch of sand that's one of the best swimming beaches on the North Shore in summer and one of the most powerful surf breaks in winter. The gradient is steep and the shore break hits hard, so even in summer, stay aware. In winter, sit on the sand and watch the professionals handle conditions that would kill most people. Parking along Kamehameha Highway.

4 Beginner Surf Lessons

The North Shore's summer waves at spots like Puaena Point (just east of Haleiwa Harbor) are actually good for beginners—small, consistent, and less crowded than Waikiki. Several North Shore surf schools operate out of Haleiwa. A 2-hour group lesson runs about $80–$100. Do not attempt to surf Pipeline, Sunset, or Waimea regardless of skill level unless you know what you're doing.

Adventures & Wildlife

5 Shark Diving

Oahu's North Shore is one of the best places in the world for cage-free shark diving. Galapagos sharks and sandbar sharks are the most common species. Boats depart from Haleiwa Harbor and head 3 miles offshore where the sharks aggregate. You're in the open ocean on a snorkel—no cage between you and 6-foot sharks circling below. It's not as dangerous as it sounds (these species rarely interact aggressively with humans), but it's an adrenaline rush.

North Shore Shark Dive

From $120

Multiple operators run cage-free snorkel encounters with Galapagos and sandbar sharks from Haleiwa Harbor. Small boats, experienced crew, safety briefing included. Free cancellation. One of Oahu's most unique experiences.

Duration: 2 hours • Departs: Haleiwa Harbor

6 Turtle Beach (Laniakea)

Hawaiian green sea turtles haul out on this beach almost daily to rest on the sand. Volunteers are usually present to keep visitors at the required 10-foot distance. The turtles are wild and their schedule varies, but late morning to early afternoon has the best odds. Parking is a pull-off along Kamehameha Highway—watch for the crowd and the turtle crossing signs. Free.

Turtle etiquette: Don't touch, approach, or block the turtles' path to the ocean. It's a federal offense (Marine Turtle Protection Act) and carries fines up to $20,000. Use a zoom lens for photos. The volunteers take it seriously.

7 Skydiving

The North Shore is one of the most scenic skydiving locations in the world—you jump over the green interior mountains and land near the coast with views of both shores. Tandem jumps require no experience. The 15,000-foot altitude gives about 60 seconds of freefall. Multiple drop zones operate near Dillingham Airfield on the far west end of the North Shore. $250–$350 per jump.

8 Waimea Valley

A 1,875-acre botanical garden and cultural preserve in the valley behind Waimea Bay. The main attraction is a paved 0.75-mile walk to Waimea Falls, a 45-foot waterfall with a swimming pool at the base—life jackets provided and required. The garden itself has over 5,000 tropical plant species and archaeological sites from ancient Hawaiian settlements. $25 adults, $14 kids. Good for families. 2–3 hours.

Food & Town

9 Haleiwa Town

Haleiwa is the only real town on the North Shore—a small main street with surf shops, art galleries, restaurants, and a harbor. It has the vibe of a surf town that hasn't been fully gentrified yet, though it's heading that way. Park once and walk. Good stops: Haleiwa Joe's (seafood with mountain views), Beet Box Cafe (açaí bowls and healthy food), and Aoki's Shave Ice or Matsumoto's for the obligatory shave ice experience.

10 Shrimp Trucks

The North Shore shrimp truck is a Hawaii institution. Giovanni's Shrimp Truck (white truck covered in signatures) at Kahuku is the most famous—the garlic scampi plate ($16) is what everyone orders. But Fumi's Kahuku Shrimp (across the road) is just as good and usually has a shorter line. The hot-and-spicy option at either is excellent. Romy's and Giovanni's also have locations near Haleiwa. Cash helps at some trucks.

Shrimp truck route: The trucks cluster in two areas: near Haleiwa on the west side, and in Kahuku on the east side. Kahuku is the original shrimp truck territory—the prawns are farmed locally in aquaculture ponds you'll drive past. Hit Kahuku on your way around the coast.

11 Matsumoto Shave Ice

Matsumoto's in Haleiwa has been serving shave ice since 1951 and draws lines that can stretch down the block. The texture is finer than mainland snow cones—more like fresh snow. Get it with ice cream on the bottom and condensed milk on top (li hing mui + mango + lychee is a local favorite combo). $4–$6. If the line is too long, Aoki's next door is nearly as good.

Nature & Scenic

12 Ehukai Pillbox Hike

A short but steep trail above Sunset Beach that leads to two WWII pillbox bunkers with panoramic North Shore views—Pipeline, Sunset Beach, and the Waianae Mountains in one sweep. About 1 mile round trip, 20–30 minutes up. The trail is muddy when wet and exposed, so go early for both cooler temps and better light. One of the best sunrise hikes on the island.

13 Ka'ena Point

The westernmost tip of Oahu, accessible only on foot—a flat 5-mile round-trip coastal trail from the North Shore side. It's a natural area reserve with nesting Laysan albatross (November–July), monk seals, and some of the most remote-feeling coastline on Oahu. No shade, no facilities—bring water and sun protection. The landscape feels more like a remote Pacific atoll than a populated island. Best in morning light.

14 Dole Plantation

The Dole Plantation is the most visited attraction on the North Shore—a pineapple-themed tourist stop with a garden maze (billed as the world's largest), a train ride through the plantation, and a gift shop. The Dole Whip (pineapple soft-serve) is the main draw. It's touristy and commercial, but the Dole Whip is genuinely good and the maze is fun for kids. $13 maze, $13 train, or $18 combo. Located on the drive between Honolulu and Haleiwa.

15 North Shore Farmers Market

The Haleiwa Farmers Market runs Saturday mornings (8am–noon) at Waimea Valley. Local produce, tropical fruits, honey, coffee, baked goods, and prepared food from local vendors. It's a good first stop on a North Shore day trip—grab breakfast and pick up fresh fruit for the car. The vibe is community-oriented, not touristy. Other markets rotate through the week at various North Shore locations.

Practical Tips

Drive early, leave early. Traffic from Honolulu to the North Shore is manageable before 9am. Returning south between 3–6pm on weekdays can take 2+ hours. Plan around it.

Route options: Take the H-2 north to Haleiwa, then drive east along Kamehameha Highway through Sunset Beach, Pipeline, and Kahuku (food trucks). This one-way loop brings you back to Honolulu via the Windward Coast for variety.

Summer vs. winter: Summer (May–September) is best for swimming, snorkeling, and water activities. Winter (November–February) is for watching world-class surfing. Both are worth it—just know what you're getting.

Cell service: Coverage is spotty between Haleiwa and Kahuku. Download offline maps before you go.
