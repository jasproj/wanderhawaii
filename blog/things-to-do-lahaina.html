15 Things to Do in Lahaina, Maui (2026)

Updated August 2026 • 10 min read

Lahaina is rebuilding. The August 2023 wildfire devastated the historic heart of West Maui's harbor town, and the recovery is real and ongoing. But Lahaina is open — the harbor runs, the boat tours run, Front Street businesses are reopening, and the community has made it clear: visiting is how you help. This guide covers what's available now, what's coming back, and why Lahaina remains the center of gravity for everything on the west side of Maui.

Note on recovery: Lahaina is actively rebuilding. Some blocks and businesses are still under construction. Check ahead for specific restaurants and shops. The harbor, boat tours, beaches, and most activities listed here are fully operational.

1. Lahaina Harbor

Lahaina Harbor is the departure point for most of West Maui's ocean activities — snorkeling trips to Molokini and Lanai, whale watching cruises, sunset sails, fishing charters, and submarine tours. The harbor is small (one main loading dock, a seawall, and a breakwater) but it handles dozens of vessels daily. Booking a morning snorkel trip or an afternoon whale watch that departs from here is how most visitors experience Lahaina's waterfront. The harbor is fully operational and has been throughout the recovery.

2. Whale Watching (December–April)

Lahaina sits on the Au'au Channel between Maui and Lanai — one of the most concentrated humpback whale grounds in the North Pacific. From December through April, an estimated 10,000 humpbacks migrate here to breed and calve in the warm, shallow water. Whale watching cruises depart from Lahaina Harbor daily, and the transit time to the whales is often under 10 minutes. Peak season is January through March. The Pacific Whale Foundation and several smaller operators offer 2-hour cruises with naturalist guides. From shore, you can often spot breaches and spouts from the seawall or any elevated point along the coast.

3. Snorkeling Trips to Molokini Crater

Molokini is a partially submerged volcanic crater about 3 miles off Maui's south shore — a crescent-shaped reef with visibility that regularly exceeds 100 feet. Most snorkeling boats depart from Ma'alaea Harbor (south of Lahaina), but several operators run from Lahaina Harbor as well, combining Molokini with a stop at Turtle Town or Coral Gardens. The inside of the crater is calm, clear, and loaded with tropical fish. Morning trips get the best visibility before the wind picks up. It's the single most popular snorkeling destination in Hawaii for a reason.

4. The Banyan Court

The Lahaina Banyan Court holds one of the largest banyan trees in the United States — planted in 1873, now covering nearly an entire city block with its aerial roots and canopy. The tree survived the 2023 fire (damaged but alive, and its recovery has become a symbol of Lahaina's own). The park around it, between Front Street and the harbor, has historically been the gathering place for the town — art fairs, festivals, and evening strolls. As Front Street rebuilds, the Banyan Court remains the anchor.

5. Front Street

Front Street was Lahaina's main commercial strip — galleries, restaurants, surf shops, and shave ice stands lining the waterfront. The 2023 fire destroyed much of it. Rebuilding is underway, and businesses are reopening in phases. The character of Front Street — walkable, waterfront, casual — is coming back. Check current openings before you go, but the rebuilt and reopened sections are worth walking. The ocean-side views toward Lanai haven't changed.

6. Sunset Sailing

A sunset sail out of Lahaina Harbor is one of the best things to do on Maui. Several catamarans and sailboats run 2-hour sunset cruises along the West Maui coast — open bar, appetizers, and the sun dropping behind Lanai while humpback whales breach in the channel (in season). Trilogy, Teralani, and Scotch Mist are established operators. The combination of the Au'au Channel, the Lanai silhouette, and the West Maui Mountains glowing at golden hour is hard to beat from any boat in Hawaii.

7. Ka'anapali Beach

Ka'anapali is the resort beach just north of Lahaina — a 3-mile stretch of white sand backed by hotels and the Whalers Village shopping center. Black Rock (Pu'u Keka'a) at the north end is the landmark: a volcanic promontory you can snorkel around (turtles, reef fish, good coral) or watch cliff divers leap from at sunset (the Sheraton's nightly torch-lighting ceremony). The beach itself is wide, swimmable, and one of the best on Maui. The Ka'anapali Beach Walk connects the hotels and makes the whole stretch walkable.

8. Old Lahaina Luau

Old Lahaina Luau is widely considered the best luau on Maui — and often the best in Hawaii. It runs on the oceanfront just south of the harbor. The food is traditional Hawaiian (kalua pig, poi, laulau, poke, haupia), the show traces Hawaiian history from Polynesian voyaging through the monarchy era, and the setting — sunset over the ocean with Lanai in the background — is exceptional. It books out weeks ahead in peak season. There are flashier luaus on Maui, but this one prioritizes authenticity over spectacle.

9. Fishing Charters

The waters off Lahaina hold some of the best sport fishing in Hawaii. The Au'au Channel and the deep water off the West Maui coast produce marlin (blue, striped, black), mahi-mahi, ono (wahoo), and yellowfin tuna. Charters run half-day and full-day trips from the harbor. The summer months (May through September) are peak marlin season. Bottom fishing trips for ulua and snapper are available year-round. Several boats offer shared charters if you don't want to book the whole vessel.

10. Lahaina Historic Trail

Before it was a tourist town, Lahaina was the capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom (1820–1845) and one of the Pacific's busiest whaling ports. The Lahaina Historic Trail is a self-guided walking tour marked by numbered plaques at significant sites — the old courthouse (now a gallery and visitor center), the prison (Hale Pa'ahao, built by the missionaries to jail unruly sailors), the site of Kamehameha III's royal residence, and the Baldwin Home Museum. Many of these sites survived or are being restored post-fire. The trail takes about an hour and gives you the context for why this small town matters to Hawaiian history.

11. Snorkeling at Honolua Bay

Honolua Bay is a marine preserve about 20 minutes north of Lahaina on the road toward Kapalua. It's a rocky bay with no beach (you enter over rocks), but the snorkeling is some of the best on Maui — clear water, healthy coral, abundant reef fish, and regular turtle sightings. In summer, the water is calm and the visibility is exceptional. In winter, the bay becomes a world-class surf break (watch from the cliffs, don't swim). No facilities — bring everything. It's the snorkeling spot locals recommend when Molokini feels too crowded.

12. Kapalua Coastal Trail

A 1.76-mile paved trail that hugs the coast from Kapalua Bay to D.T. Fleming Beach, about 15 minutes north of Lahaina. The trail passes through ironwood groves, over lava headlands, and along cliff edges with views of Molokai across the channel. In whale season, you'll spot breaches from the trail. Both beaches at either end are excellent — Kapalua Bay is calm and sheltered (great for kids and snorkeling), D.T. Fleming is a wider, wilder beach with stronger surf. Walk one direction and Uber back, or do the round trip.

13. Submarine Tour

Atlantis Submarines operates out of Lahaina Harbor — a real submarine that dives to about 100 feet off the coast. You descend in a 48-passenger sub and view coral reefs, tropical fish, and a sunken ship through large viewports. It's not snorkeling and it's not a glass-bottom boat — it's an actual submarine dive. It's a good option for people who want to see the reef but don't want to get in the water (non-swimmers, young kids, older visitors). The dive itself is about 45 minutes.

14. Road to Hana (from West Maui)

Most people drive the Road to Hana from Kahului, but you can also start from West Maui, which adds about 30 minutes to the drive. The advantage: you're already on the scenic side of the island and can stop at Ho'okipa Beach (turtle watching, world-class windsurfing) and Twin Falls early. Guided tours depart from Lahaina and Ka'anapali with hotel pickup. The Road to Hana is 64 miles of winding road, 600+ curves, 59 bridges, and waterfalls, bamboo forests, and black sand beaches along the way. Full-day commitment.

15. Fleetwood's on Front Street

Mick Fleetwood's rooftop restaurant and live music venue on Front Street — one of Lahaina's signature dining experiences. The rooftop terrace has panoramic views of the harbor, Lanai, and the West Maui Mountains. Live music most nights. The menu is upscale Hawaiian-fusion. Even if you don't eat here, the rooftop bar at sunset is worth the stop. Check whether it has reopened at its original location or a temporary venue — Lahaina's restaurant scene is in flux as rebuilding continues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Lahaina open to visitors after the 2023 fire?

Yes. The harbor is operational, boat tours run full schedules, beaches are open, and Front Street businesses are reopening in phases. Some areas are still under construction. Visiting supports the recovery — the community has been clear about that.

When is whale watching season in Lahaina?

December through April. Humpback whales migrate to the Au'au Channel off Lahaina to breed and calve. Peak sightings are January through March. Cruises depart from Lahaina Harbor daily in season.

What is Lahaina known for?

Its history as the capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom and a 19th-century whaling port, the Banyan Court tree, Front Street, Lahaina Harbor as the departure point for snorkeling and whale watching, and the Old Lahaina Luau. It's the commercial center of West Maui.

Is Lahaina or Kihei better for whale watching?

Both work, but Lahaina is the traditional hub. The Au'au Channel directly off Lahaina Harbor is one of the densest humpback grounds in Hawaii, so transit time to the whales is short — often under 10 minutes.
