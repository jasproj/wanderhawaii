12 Best Hikes on Oahu in 2026

Updated August 2026 • 14 min read

Oahu has more hikeable terrain per square mile than any other Hawaiian island. The Ko'olau mountain range runs the length of the windward side, and the Waianae range frames the west coast—between them are ridgelines, waterfalls, volcanic craters, and coastal trails that range from paved walkways to exposed scrambles where people die every year.

This guide covers 12 hikes from tourist-friendly to genuinely challenging, with honest assessments of what each requires. All distances are round trip.

Easy Hikes

1 Diamond Head Summit Trail Easy

The most famous hike in Hawaii. A 1.6-mile round trip through a volcanic crater to the summit with 560 feet of elevation gain. The panoramic view of Waikiki and the south shore is the payoff—and it delivers. The trail includes two sets of stairs (99 steps and 54 steps) and a short tunnel. Takes 1–1.5 hours total.

Distance: 1.6 mi RT • Elevation: 560 ft • Time: 1–1.5 hours
Access: Reservation required ($5/person) at hawaiistateparks.org. Opens at 6am. Parking fills by 8:30am—Uber from Waikiki or walk (2 miles from central Waikiki to the crater entrance).

2 Makapuu Lighthouse Trail Easy

A paved 2-mile round trip along the southeast coast with views of the Windward side, Rabbit Island, and (in winter) humpback whales breaching offshore. The grade is gentle enough for strollers and wheelchairs for the first half. The lighthouse itself is a red-roofed beauty on a cliff. Best whale watching on Oahu from December through April.

Distance: 2 mi RT • Elevation: 500 ft • Time: 1 hour
Access: Free. Parking lot off Kalanianaole Highway (Hwy 72) near Makapuu Beach. No reservation needed. Go early for parking and whale watching.

3 Manoa Falls Trail Easy

A 1.6-mile round trip through a rainforest valley to a 150-foot waterfall. The trail is flat and shaded—the challenge is mud, not distance. After rain (which is frequent in Manoa Valley), the trail becomes a slippery mess. Proper shoes are non-negotiable. The waterfall itself is impressive after rain. Swimming at the base is officially discouraged due to leptospirosis risk in the pool.

Distance: 1.6 mi RT • Elevation: 800 ft • Time: 1–1.5 hours
Access: $5 parking fee at the trailhead (exact cash or card). Located at the back of Manoa Valley, 15 minutes from Waikiki. Go in the morning—afternoon rain makes the trail worse and the valley clouds over.

Moderate Hikes

4 Lanikai Pillbox (Kaiwa Ridge) Moderate

A short, steep climb to two WWII pillbox bunkers overlooking Lanikai Beach and the Mokulua Islands. The view from the first pillbox is one of the most photographed on Oahu—turquoise water, offshore islands, and the Kailua coastline. Popular sunrise hike. The trail is only 1.8 miles round trip but the initial climb is steep and the trail is eroded in places.

Distance: 1.8 mi RT • Elevation: 600 ft • Time: 1–1.5 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead is on Kaelepulu Drive in Lanikai (residential neighborhood). Street parking is extremely limited—go at sunrise or take an Uber.

5 Koko Head Stairs Moderate

1,048 railroad-tie steps straight up the side of Koko Head crater. No switchbacks, no shade, no mercy. It's the stairmaster from hell—but the view from the top of the southeast coast is excellent, and finishing it feels like an accomplishment. The notorious "bridge" section (where ties span a gap over a gully) can be bypassed on a side trail. Extremely popular with locals as a morning workout.

Distance: 1.8 mi RT • Elevation: 1,050 ft • Time: 45 min–1.5 hours
Access: Free. Located inside Koko Head District Park off Lunalilo Home Road. Go at sunrise—the stairs bake in full sun by mid-morning and it becomes a heat-exhaustion risk. Bring at least 1 liter of water.
Koko Head honesty: Most healthy adults can finish this hike, but it's more intense than it looks. People regularly quit halfway or need medical attention from dehydration. If 1,000 stairs sounds like a lot, it is. There's no shame in turning around.

6 Aiea Loop Trail Moderate

A 4.8-mile loop through forest above Pearl Harbor with views of the harbor, Diamond Head, and the Ko'olau range. The trail passes a WWII-era plane crash site (wing fragments visible off-trail). Mostly shaded, rolling terrain with moderate elevation changes. Less crowded than the popular south shore hikes—feels like you're actually in the mountains, not on a tourist path.

Distance: 4.8 mi loop • Elevation: 900 ft • Time: 2–3 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead at Keaiwa Heiau State Recreation Area above Aiea. 20 minutes from Waikiki. Park gate hours apply—check before going.

7 Kuliouou Ridge Trail Moderate

A steady climb from sea level to the Ko'olau ridgeline with a panoramic summit view of both the windward and leeward coasts. The trail starts in a residential neighborhood and climbs through forest to an exposed ridge. The final push to the summit requires some light scrambling but nothing technical. One of the best summit-to-ocean views on Oahu.

Distance: 5 mi RT • Elevation: 1,800 ft • Time: 3–4 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead at the end of Kalaau Place in Hawaii Kai. Limited street parking—go early. 20 minutes from Waikiki.

8 Ehukai Pillbox (North Shore) Moderate

Two WWII bunkers above Sunset Beach with sweeping North Shore views. Shorter and steeper than Lanikai Pillbox, with a better payoff if you time it with big winter surf below. About 1 mile round trip, 20–30 minutes up. The trail is exposed and can be muddy. Excellent at sunrise or sunset. See our North Shore guide for more.

Distance: 1 mi RT • Elevation: 450 ft • Time: 30–45 min
Access: Free. Trailhead off Sunset Point neighborhood near Sunset Beach. 45 min from Waikiki.

Hard Hikes

9 Olomana Three Peaks Hard

The most dangerous legal hike on Oahu. Three peaks connected by narrow ridgelines with sheer drops on both sides. The first peak is challenging but manageable; the second requires ropes; the third is a knife-edge scramble where people have fallen to their deaths. Most hikers stop at the first peak (and should). The views of the windward coast are spectacular—the risk is real.

Distance: 4.5 mi RT (all 3 peaks) • Elevation: 1,600 ft • Time: 4–6 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead off Auloa Road near Maunawili. Not for beginners, not in wet conditions, and not alone. Check trip reports before going.

10 Ka'ena Point (North Shore side) Moderate

A flat but long coastal walk to Oahu's westernmost tip—a natural area reserve with nesting albatross and monk seals. The terrain is rocky and exposed with zero shade. The distance is the challenge, not the elevation. One of the most remote-feeling spots on an otherwise urban island. Best November through July when albatross are nesting.

Distance: 5 mi RT • Elevation: minimal • Time: 2.5–3.5 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead at the end of Farrington Highway past Mokule'ia. Bring at least 2 liters of water and sun protection—there is no shade on the entire trail.

11 Wiliwilinui Ridge Trail Moderate

A well-maintained ridge trail that climbs through native forest to a Ko'olau summit viewpoint. Less crowded than Kuliouou with a similar caliber view. The trail passes through a residential neighborhood and a golf course before entering forest. Final section is a metal staircase to the ridgeline. Clear-day views extend to Molokai.

Distance: 4.4 mi RT • Elevation: 1,500 ft • Time: 3–4 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead via Laukahi Street in Waialae Iki. Limited parking—carpool or Uber. 20 minutes from Waikiki.

12 Lulumahu Falls Moderate

A 2-mile round trip through dense forest to a secluded 50-foot waterfall. The trail crosses a stream multiple times and is perpetually muddy—waterproof shoes or old sneakers you don't mind ruining. The falls are hidden in a narrow valley surrounded by dense vegetation. One of the more atmospheric hikes on Oahu, especially after rain when the falls are flowing strong.

Distance: 2 mi RT • Elevation: 700 ft • Time: 1.5–2 hours
Access: Free. Trailhead off Pali Highway near the Old Pali Road. Requires walking through a residential area and past a water treatment facility. The trail is unofficial and unmarked—GPS track recommended.

General Hiking Tips for Oahu

Start early. 6:30–7:00am for most hikes. Cooler temperatures, better parking, fewer people, and you finish before afternoon rain rolls in.

Mud is the constant. Oahu trails are muddy year-round, especially on the windward side and in valleys. Hiking shoes with real traction are worth the suitcase space. Sandals and fashion sneakers cause most trail injuries here.

Flash flood awareness: Never hike in a valley or near a stream during heavy rain. Flash floods kill people in Hawaii every year, and they arrive with little warning. If it starts raining hard, get to high ground.

Don't hike illegal trails. Some of Oahu's most instagrammed hikes (Stairway to Heaven/Haiku Stairs, Lulumahu in some interpretations) exist in legal gray areas or have been formally closed. Respect closures—they exist because people have been injured or killed. Fines for trespassing on closed trails can reach $1,000+.

Leave valuables in your hotel. Car break-ins at trailhead parking lots are common on Oahu, especially at Manoa Falls, Lanikai Pillbox, and Makapuu. Don't leave anything visible in the car.
