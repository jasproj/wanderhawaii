Hawaii vs Puerto Rico: Volcanoes, Bio Bays & Which to Pick

Updated August 2026 | ~3 min read

Two tropical U.S. destinations, zero passport required. Hawaii offers volcanic landscapes, world-class surfing, and manta rays. Puerto Rico offers bioluminescent bays, colonial history, and a shorter flight from the East Coast. Here is how they stack up.

Overall: Hawaii runs an estimated 20–40% more than Puerto Rico for flights, hotels, and dining. A mid-range week in Hawaii: $1,800–2,400. A week in Puerto Rico: $1,200–1,800. West Coast travelers save on flights to Hawaii; East Coast travelers save on flights to Puerto Rico.

What Hawaii Has That Puerto Rico Doesn’t

  • Active volcanoes — Kilauea on the Big Island is one of the world’s most active. Walk across lava fields and see volcanic craters up close.
  • Manta ray night snorkeling — Big Island’s Kona coast offers swimming with 12-foot manta rays after dark. No equivalent exists anywhere in the Caribbean.
  • Na Pali Coast — Kauai’s 17-mile sea cliff coastline, accessible only by boat, helicopter, or an 11-mile trail.
  • North Shore surfing — Oahu’s winter waves are legendary. Pipeline, Sunset Beach, and Waimea Bay draw the world’s best surfers.
  • Island diversity — Six visitable islands, each with a distinct personality, from Maui’s beaches to Molokai’s solitude.

What Puerto Rico Has That Hawaii Doesn’t

  • Bioluminescent bays — Three permanent bays where microscopic organisms light up the water. Mosquito Bay on Vieques is one of the brightest in the world. Hawaii has no equivalent. Learn about the bio bays →
  • 500 years of colonial history — Old San Juan’s forts, cobblestone streets, and pastel buildings predate the Pilgrims by over a century.
  • El Yunque — The only tropical rainforest in the U.S. National Forest system, 45 minutes from San Juan.
  • Shorter flights from the East Coast — 3–5 hours vs 8–10 hours, with more direct routes and cheaper fares.
  • Latin Caribbean culture — Salsa, reggaetón, mofongo, and rum in a setting that feels international without leaving the U.S.

Beaches

Hawaii: Black-sand beaches (Punalu’u), red-sand (Kaihalulu), white sand (Lanikai, Ka’anapali). Surf breaks on the North Shore, calm bays on leeward coasts. Sea turtles at Laniakea.

Puerto Rico: Over 300 beaches. Flamenco Beach on Culebra consistently ranks in the world’s top 5. Golden sand at Luquillo, surf at Rincón, and secluded coves on Vieques. See Puerto Rico’s best beaches →

Snorkeling

Hawaii wins on diversity: More reef species, deeper volcanic drop-offs, manta ray encounters, and spinner dolphin sightings. Hanauma Bay on Oahu and Molokini Crater off Maui are world-class.

Puerto Rico wins on ease: Calmer waters, easier shore entry, and excellent visibility in protected lagoons. Great for beginners and families.

Weather

Hawaii: 70–85°F year-round. Drier summers, wetter winters (especially windward sides). More predictable and milder overall. No real hurricane threat.

Puerto Rico: 75–88°F year-round. Dry season Dec–Apr, wet season May–Nov. Hurricane season June–Nov but direct hits are rare.

Food & Culture

Hawaii: Poke bowls, plate lunches, shave ice, Kalua pig. A blend of Polynesian, Japanese, Filipino, and American influences. Luaus are a must-do cultural experience.

Puerto Rico: Mofongo, lechon asado, alcapurrias, fresh seafood. Spanish-Caribbean-African fusion. La Placita night market in Santurce. Bacardi and Don Q rum tours. Explore Puerto Rico tours →

Which to Choose

Choose Hawaii if: You want volcanoes and lava landscapes, world-class surfing, manta ray encounters, diverse island-hopping, or you’re on the West Coast and want a shorter flight.

Choose Puerto Rico if: Budget matters, you want bioluminescent bays, you prefer colonial history and Latin culture, you’re on the East Coast, or you want a Caribbean feel without leaving the U.S. Read the Puerto Rico perspective →

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Hawaii or Puerto Rico more expensive? Hawaii is generally 20–40% more expensive. Flights from the mainland cost $300–600 vs $150–300 for Puerto Rico, and hotels and dining run higher across all islands.

Which has better snorkeling? Both are excellent. Hawaii has more reef diversity, manta ray night snorkeling, and deeper volcanic drop-offs. Puerto Rico has calmer lagoons and easier shore-entry snorkeling.

Does Hawaii have bioluminescent bays? No. Hawaii gets occasional faint plankton-bloom sightings, but Puerto Rico has three permanent bioluminescent bays where the water glows brightly when disturbed.

Do I need a passport for either? No. Both are U.S. territories — fly domestic with a driver’s license from the mainland.

What can you do in Hawaii that you can’t do in Puerto Rico? Active volcano hiking at Kilauea, manta ray night snorkeling on the Big Island, surfing the North Shore of Oahu, and the Na Pali Coast on Kauai.
