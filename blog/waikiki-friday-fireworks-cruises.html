Waikiki Friday Night Fireworks: Times, Free Spots and Every Cruise Compared

Every Friday night, the Hilton Hawaiian Village fires a five-minute fireworks show from its lagoon at the west end of Waikiki Beach. It starts around 7:45 p.m. most of the year — closer to 8 in summer, a little earlier in the short days of winter — and it is free from the sand. The catch is the sand: by 7:30 the beach from the Hilton to Fort DeRussy is shoulder to shoulder.

The boats solve that. Roughly a dozen operators run Friday-only sunset cruises that leave from Kewalo Basin or the Hilton pier around 6 p.m., sail the Waikiki shoreline through sunset, and hold position off the lagoon for the show. Prices start at $64 per person. Here is what each one is, what you get for the money, and how to choose.

When and where

  • Day: every Friday, year-round; occasionally cancelled for high wind or a holiday week
  • Time: about 7:45 p.m.; roughly 8 p.m. in summer, 7–7:45 in winter. Confirm with your operator the week of.
  • Length: about five minutes
  • Launch point: the Hilton Hawaiian Village lagoon, west end of Waikiki
  • Boats depart: Kewalo Basin (10 minutes west of Waikiki by car) or the Hilton pier; check the listing — it decides your evening's logistics

Cheapest way on the water: BYOB catamarans

Three operators run the same format at the same price point: a sailing catamaran, bring your own drinks, about two hours, sunset then fireworks. They differ mainly in departure point and headcount.

How to choose

  • On a budget: any of the three BYOB catamarans at $64–$69. Bring a cooler bag and walk on.
  • Staying in Waikiki without a car: Pink Sails or Aloha Sails — you board from the sand.
  • Making it the evening out: Port Waikiki's Spirit of Aloha or Ocean Therapy; both have a bar and a longer sail.
  • Group of 4–6: the private charter works out to about $200 a head and you set the schedule.
  • Prone to seasickness: the water off Waikiki is usually calm; catamarans are steadier than the monohull powerboats. Sit aft, watch the horizon.

Whatever you book, be at the dock 30 minutes before departure. Friday boats leave on time because the show does.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time are the Friday fireworks in Waikiki?

Around 7:45 p.m. every Friday — closer to 8 p.m. in summer and as early as 7 p.m. in midwinter. The show is launched from the Hilton Hawaiian Village lagoon and lasts about five minutes.

Are the Waikiki Friday fireworks free?

Yes, from the beach. The best free spots are the sand between the Hilton lagoon and Fort DeRussy and the Ala Wai Harbor breakwater; arrive by 7:15 for a clear view. Boat cruises that position off the lagoon start at about $64 per person.

How much is a Waikiki fireworks cruise?

BYOB catamaran sails cost $64–$69 per person; beach-boarding catamarans with a bar run $79–$104; dinner-and-bar cruises are $160–$175; a private boat for up to six is about $1,200.

Where do the fireworks cruises leave from?

Most leave from Kewalo Basin, about ten minutes west of Waikiki by car or rideshare. Pink Sails and Aloha Sails board directly from Waikiki Beach in front of the Hilton and Sheraton.

Are the Friday fireworks ever cancelled?

Occasionally, for strong winds or during some holiday weeks. Operators know by the afternoon; if the show is cancelled the boats still run as a sunset sail or offer a reschedule.
