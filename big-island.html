Big Island Hawaii, region by region

The Big Island (Hawaiʻi Island) is larger than all the other Hawaiian islands combined — 4,028 square miles, with two sides that feel like different countries. Kona on the west is dry, sunny and where the boats leave from; Hilo on the east is wet, green and the gateway to the volcano. Driving between them takes about 2 hours over Saddle Road. Below: what to do in each region, with tours you can book now. Full lists in our 25 best things to do on the Big Island, things to do in Kona and things to do in Hilo.

Kona & the west coast

Kailua-Kona is the tour hub: snorkel boats to Kealakekua Bay (Captain Cook Monument), dolphin and sail trips out of Honokōhau Harbor, and coffee farms up the slopes of Hualālai. Water visibility runs 60–100 ft most mornings, so book the early departures. Kona Snorkel Trips is our 2026 Golden Lei nominee for Big Island snorkeling.

The signature Big Island experience: float on a surfboard-style raft at night off Keauhou or Makako Bay while mantas with 12-ft wingspans loop under the lights, feeding on plankton. Frequently asked questions

What is the Big Island of Hawaii known for?

Active volcanoes (Kīlauea and Mauna Loa in Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park), the manta ray night snorkel off Kona, Mauna Kea's 13,803-ft summit and stargazing, Kona coffee, black and green sand beaches, and some of the clearest snorkeling water in the state at Kealakekua Bay.

What are the best things to do on the Big Island?

Snorkel Kealakekua Bay, do the manta ray night snorkel, visit Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park at dusk, go up to the Mauna Kea visitor station for stars, see Akaka Falls near Hilo, tour a Kona coffee farm, and swim at Hāpuna Beach. Our 25-item list links every tour you can book.

Is Kona or Hilo better to stay on the Big Island?

Kona for sun, beaches and most boat tours; Hilo for waterfalls, rainforest and a 45-minute drive to the volcano. Most visitors base in Kona or Waikoloa and do the volcano as a day trip; two-base trips (3 nights each side) work well for a week.

How many days do you need on the Big Island?

Four to five days covers Kona snorkeling, a manta night, the volcano and Mauna Kea; a week lets you add the Hilo side and the south beaches without rushing. Day-tripping from another island is possible but you will spend most of the day in transit.

When is the best time to visit the Big Island?

Kona is good year-round. April–May and September–October have the fewest crowds and lower prices; December–March brings humpback whales offshore and the highest resort rates. Mantas are present every month.

Can you see lava on the Big Island?

Only when Kīlauea is erupting, which happens in episodes rather than continuously. Check the National Park Service's current-conditions page the day you go. Guided volcano tours adjust the route to whatever is visible.
